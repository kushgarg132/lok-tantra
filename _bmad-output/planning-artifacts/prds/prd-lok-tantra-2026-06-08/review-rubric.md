# PRD Quality Review — Power Explorer (LokTantra)

## Overall verdict
This is a tightly-scoped, well-sequenced PRD that knows what it's betting on (graph navigation + constitutional grounding + time-travel as one unified "Constitution in motion" experience) and is honest about what it's deferring and why. Its strongest asset is scope honesty — the MVP slice, non-goals, and assumptions are explicit and traceable to the decision log. Its weakest spot is done-ness clarity for the feature that carries the most technical risk (Time Travel/FR-7) and for cross-cutting NFRs (neutrality, performance) that read as commitments but lack the bounds an engineer or QA would need to call them satisfied — plus one unresolved naming/identity question (the working title) that a launch-grade PRD shouldn't carry forward.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The PRD states real decisions as decisions rather than burying them: the MVP slice (national/central layer only, deferring states/MPs/MLAs/Activity Data) is framed candidly as a sequencing call the PM made and the user approved (decision log, MVP slice section), and §8.2 names each deferral with a reason ("the heaviest ongoing data-sourcing and maintenance lift of everything discussed" for Activity Data). The `[NOTE FOR PM]` at §8.2 on Snapshot/bookmark — "this was the emotional payoff of Priya's journey (UJ-2) — worth revisiting" — is a real tension named honestly, not a safe checkpoint: it admits the MVP cuts something the PRD's own UJ built emotional weight around.

Two things hold this back from "strong." First, Open Question #3 (historical data availability back to 1947) is framed as "the single biggest determinant of whether FR-7 is buildable as scoped" — which is the right instinct to surface it, but a launch-grade PRD that calls a feature's buildability into question in its own Open Questions section is, by definition, not yet decision-ready on its headline capability (Time Travel is the feature's most differentiated claim per SM-3). The PRD is honest about this risk but doesn't resolve or even bound it (e.g., "if data isn't available pre-1991, what's the fallback scope?"). Second, the working title itself is unresolved — "*Working title — confirm.*" sits directly under the H1 (line 9) of a document whose front-matter and §0 otherwise present as final ("status: draft" but described to stakeholders as the launch-grade spine). That's a small thing to fix but a strange thing to ship un-decided in the artifact that's supposed to anchor downstream naming (the Glossary, the IA replacement of `/power-structure`, etc., all assume "Power Explorer").

### Findings
- **[high]** Time Travel buildability is an open question, not a decision (§10 OQ-3, §4.4/FR-7) — The PRD's most differentiated capability ("the feature's most differentiated capability and the heart of the 'Constitution in motion' promise," per SM-3) is explicitly flagged as possibly not buildable as scoped, with no fallback stated if 1947-depth data doesn't exist. *Fix:* Add a stated fallback (e.g., "if verified Tenure data isn't available before year X, Time Travel ships bounded to that year for v1, with 1947 as a stretch goal") so the PRD survives a "no" answer to OQ-3 without a re-scope cycle.
- **[medium]** Working title left unconfirmed in a launch-grade artifact (line 9, "*Working title — confirm.*") — Every downstream section (Glossary, IA replacement plan, Vision) is written as if "Power Explorer" is final, yet the title carries an explicit unresolved flag. *Fix:* Either confirm the name now (it's used consistently and well throughout) or convert this into a tracked Open Question with an owner and resolve-by point — don't leave a naming decision dangling under the H1 of a document meant to anchor downstream work.

## Substance over theater — strong
No persona bloat (exactly two UJs, each driving specific FR consequences — UJ-1 → FR-1/FR-2/FR-6, UJ-2 → FR-3/FR-7), no boilerplate NFRs (the Neutrality guardrails in §5 are product-specific — "never editorially curated by party, popularity, or current events" — not generic "must be secure/scalable" filler), and the Vision (§1) is anchored in a specific insight ("Civics is usually taught as something to memorize... LokTantra's Power Explorer replaces memorization with discovery") that couldn't swap into an unrelated PRD without rewriting. The Glossary term "Tenure" — described as "the atomic unit that makes Time Travel possible" — is a genuine modeling insight earned by the feature, not a definitional formality.

The one place this dimension brushes against theater is the Differentiation framing embedded in §4.3 ("This is the throughline that fulfills the Vision's promise to 'make the Constitution easy to understand'") — it's *asserted* as the throughline rather than shown to be one relative to alternatives LokTantra already has (`/constitution` explorer, `PoliticalRelationshipMap`). That's a minor rhetorical flourish, not a structural problem, and doesn't rise to a finding-worthy flag on its own.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit and the features visibly serve it: graph navigation (4.1) + profiles (4.2) + constitutional grounding (4.3) + time travel (4.4) are bound together by one throughline — "the Constitution stops being an abstract document and becomes the living skeleton of a structure you can actually see move" (§1) — and FR-3's design choice (tapping a career-path step jumps Time Travel to that period, "the profile is a doorway into the living graph, not a disconnected mini-timeline") is a concrete case of a feature decision *following from* the thesis rather than being bolted on. The MVP scope kind is coherent — this is an "experience" MVP (the decision log explicitly frames the slice as "delivers the *complete* core experience... at a buildable scope; every later wave extends the same shape") — and the scope logic (national layer first, same shape scaled later) matches that kind cleanly.

Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring raw activity: SM-3 (Time Travel engagement — "% of sessions that move the scrubber at least once") measures whether the differentiated capability is actually used, and the counter-metrics are the strongest part of this section — SM-C1 explicitly rejects time-on-page as a vanity metric ("This product's mission is 'understand, then move on,' not 'stay scrolling'"), and SM-C2 ties growth discipline directly back to the Neutrality guardrail in §5. This is exactly the "counter-metrics named when SMs exist" the rubric asks for, done with teeth rather than as a checkbox.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Most FRs carry genuinely testable consequences — FR-2's "Expanding a Node reveals all of its direct Edges, each labeled with the relationship type" and FR-5's "Missing Accountability Data is shown as an explicit 'not available' state, never silently omitted" are exactly the verifiable conditions the rubric wants, and they'd translate cleanly into acceptance criteria. But the dimension drops to "thin" on the FRs and cross-cutting requirements that matter most for engineering estimation and QA sign-off:

- FR-1 and FR-7 both lean on "within LokTantra's existing performance expectations" / "within the same interactivity budget as the initial graph load" — this is the adjective problem the rubric calls out by name ("'reasonable performance'... flag every one"), compounded by the fact that the PRD itself flags the number as TBD (`[ASSUMPTION: specific budget TBD with architecture]`, OQ-1). A testable consequence that defers its own threshold to a later phase isn't yet testable — it's a placeholder for one.
- FR-7's "last known state" rule is well-specified as a *resolution* rule, but the FR has no testable consequence for *transition correctness* — e.g., what happens at the exact boundary date when a Tenure starts/ends, or whether two simultaneous transitions (e.g., a reshuffle moving three ministers in one day) render atomically. For the feature billed as the "heart of the 'Constitution in motion' promise," this is a meaningful gap an engineer would have to invent an answer to.
- The Neutrality guardrails (§5) are written with real specificity ("determined structurally or chronologically... never editorially curated") but have no testable consequence of their own — there's no FR or acceptance criterion that says how a reviewer or automated check would verify a given graph layout *complies* with structural-only ordering. Given §5 calls this "non-negotiable," the absence of any verification mechanism (manual review checklist? automated linter on layout rules? design review gate?) is a real gap between stated importance and operational teeth.

### Findings
- **[high]** Performance consequences defer their own threshold (FR-1, FR-7; §4.1, §4.4, OQ-1) — Both FRs state a testable consequence ("within the same interactivity budget...") whose actual bound is `[ASSUMPTION: TBD with architecture]`. As written, no one can mark these consequences pass/fail today. *Fix:* Either propose a provisional number now (e.g., "interactive within 3s on a mid-range mobile device on 4G" — even as a placeholder marked `[ASSUMPTION: confirm with architecture]`) so downstream has something to estimate against, or explicitly mark FR-1/FR-7 as "acceptance criteria pending architecture sign-off on OQ-1" so the gap is visible rather than silently inherited.
- **[medium]** Time Travel has no testable consequence for transition-boundary correctness (§4.4, FR-7) — The "last known state" rule covers in-between dates but not the moment a Tenure starts/ends or concurrent multi-Office transitions (e.g., a cabinet reshuffle). This is exactly the scenario UJ-1 centers on ("the Cabinet reshuffle everyone's talking about"), so the FR that should anticipate it most carries the least precision about it. *Fix:* Add a consequence such as "On the exact date a Tenure begins, the new occupant is shown; multiple same-day transitions render atomically as a single consistent graph state — never a partial mix of old and new occupants."
- **[medium]** Neutrality guardrails have no verification mechanism (§5) — The guardrails are specific and rightly called "non-negotiable," but nothing in the PRD says how compliance would be checked — no acceptance criterion, review gate, or automated check is named. *Fix:* Add a consequence or process note, e.g., "Layout/ordering logic is reviewable against a structural-only ruleset before each data refresh; any manual override requires PM sign-off and a logged rationale" — giving the guardrail the same operational teeth as its stated importance.

## Scope honesty — strong
This is the PRD's best dimension. Every major omission is called out explicitly and indexed: §6.2 lists three `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` items with reasons (state governments "deferred to expansion wave 2 (same shape, wider data)"; Activity Data flagged as "the heaviest ongoing data-sourcing and maintenance lift of everything discussed"), and the snapshot/bookmark deferral carries a `[NOTE FOR PM]` that names the emotional cost honestly rather than hiding it ("this was the emotional payoff of Priya's journey (UJ-2)"). The `[ASSUMPTION]` density (3 tags, all indexed in §11, all traced to specific Open Questions) is proportionate to a launch-grade PRD — not so many that it reads as unfinished, not so few that real uncertainty is being smoothed over. De-scoping is documented as a deliberate PM call with user sign-off (decision log: "User explicitly delegated the sequencing call... PM's call, user-approved"), which is exactly the "de-scoping proposed honestly, not done silently" the rubric asks for.

The open-items count (5 Open Questions + 3 `[ASSUMPTION]`s + 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]`s = 10) is on the higher side for a document presented as launch-grade/"actually launch," but each item is substantive rather than padding — OQ-3 (data availability) in particular is the kind of question that *should* surface before architecture, not after. This density is a feature of the PRD's honesty, not a symptom of incompleteness, though a reader could reasonably ask whether OQ-1/OQ-3 (performance budget, data availability) should have been resolved — or at least provisionally bounded — before calling the spine "complete" (see Done-ness and Decision-readiness findings above, which is where this tension actually bites).

## Downstream usability — adequate
The mechanics are mostly clean: the Glossary (§3) is used consistently and capitalized identically across FRs and UJs (Node, Edge, Tenure, Office, Person all appear with matching capitalization in §4's FR text), FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous (FR-1 through FR-8, UJ-1/UJ-2, SM-1 through SM-4 plus SM-C1/SM-C2), and cross-references resolve — FR-3 → "§4.4," FR-6 → "/constitution explorer," §8.2 → "§6.2" all point to real sections (modulo the §6.2 numbering issue noted in Mechanical notes below). UJs each have named, contextualized protagonists (Aman, 22, final-year student; Priya, 35, schoolteacher) carrying their context inline rather than floating.

The dimension lands at "adequate" rather than "strong" for two reasons that matter for a chain-top PRD (this one explicitly feeds UX, architecture, and epics per §0): first, FR-8's "current graph view" and "currently selected Time Travel date" phrases reference UI state that the Glossary doesn't define as a noun (no "Time Travel date" or "graph view" Glossary entry), which is a small but real seam for a UX workflow trying to source-extract state-management requirements cleanly. Second, §6's `[NOTE FOR PM]` about the migration plan being "carried into §9 Open Questions" is actually in §10 — a cross-reference that doesn't resolve as written (see Mechanical notes).

## Shape fit — strong
This is a consumer-facing, multi-persona civic product with meaningful UX, and the PRD treats it that way: two UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing (each maps directly to specific FRs, as the rubric wants), not decorative. The PRD correctly resists over-formalizing — it doesn't manufacture a UJ for every FR (FR-4, FR-5, FR-8 are referenced through UJ-1/UJ-2's arcs rather than spawning their own personas), keeping persona count at exactly two, which the rubric flags as the right ceiling. The brownfield handling is accurate and well-distinguished: the decision log and §6 correctly characterize the existing `/power-structure` route and `PowerHierarchyExplorer` component as "a static institutional tree showing only current office-holders" (verified against the actual codebase — `src/app/power-structure/page.tsx` and `src/components/power-structure/PowerHierarchyExplorer.tsx` exist and the PRD's characterization of what they lack — historical time-travel, full personal drill-down — matches), and the "fully replaces" decision is stated as a decision with the migration question correctly deferred to architecture rather than glossed over. The chain-top framing (§0: "written for the product owner... and for the downstream UX, architecture, and epics/stories workflows") is matched by the PRD's traceability apparatus (global FR numbering, Glossary discipline, Assumptions Index) — the form fits the function.
